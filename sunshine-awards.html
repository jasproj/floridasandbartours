The 2026 Sunshine Awards

Every year we look at the Florida operators listed on this site and recognise the ones doing the most for the people who book them. These are the 30 nominees for 2026.

How nominees were chosen

Nominations are drawn from our own catalogue, not from a public vote or a paid entry. Operators are shortlisted on review standing, review volume and how well they fit a category we want to cover properly. It is an editorial choice made by this site — there is no panel and no public vote, and we would rather say so than dress it up. It is recognition from this site, not from an industry body.

Nominees and winners are decided differently. The list below is editorial. Winners are scored on the published rubric underneath it.

  • No operator paid to be nominated, and none can.
  • Nomination does not affect where an operator appears in our search results.
  • Tour counts are what we list and could actually be booked when we last checked — retired and sold-out listings are not counted.
  • Figures move as operators add and drop trips, so they are re-measured rather than fixed. Last measured 4 October 2026, across 427 bookable tours.

How winners are scored

Every operator we list runs on FareHarbor, which means we can check something most review sites cannot: whether a tour is actually bookable right now, at the price shown. That check is the backbone of this rubric.

What has to be true before an operator is scored at all

  • A published rating of 4.3 or better, where the operator publishes one.
  • At least one of the tours we feature is bookable.
  • Availability on at least half the dates we check.
  • A standing signal we can actually read — a category rank in a field of 20 or more, or a review count. An operator we cannot measure is reported as unscored rather than given a low score.

The score

70% standing, 30% booking integrity.

  • Standing is the operator's Tripadvisor category rank as a percentile, where the category holds at least 20 listings. Below that we use review volume on a log scale, floored at 50 reviews and capped at 5,000 — 20,000 reviews is not twenty times better than 1,000.
  • Booking integrity is half how many of the tours we feature are bookable, and half how consistently they are available across four dates in the following eleven weeks.

Why star ratings are not part of the ranking

Across the 99 operators in our network that publish a rating, the ratings run from 4.3 to 5.0, with a median of 4.9. There is no real spread to rank on, and the underlying numbers move: two readings of the same Tripadvisor listing on the same day returned 4.4 and 4.6. Ranking operators on a tenth of a star would be inventing precision that is not there. A rating has to clear our floor, and after that it does not move anyone up or down.

Why we check a near window, not a far one

We check availability over the next eleven weeks rather than the next year. An earlier version of this rubric looked six months out and mistook unopened calendars for cancelled tours — plenty of operators simply do not open bookings that far ahead. Penalising an operator for a calendar setting would be measuring us, not them.

What we do not score

Catalogue size does not earn points. A single-boat operator running one trip well is not worse than one listing eighty products, and scoring breadth would just reward size. We report how many tours an operator lists; we do not rank on it.

Any operator we list can ask for their own score and the figures behind it, and we will send both.

  3. One winner per category is named at the end of the 2026 season, scored on the rubric above.
