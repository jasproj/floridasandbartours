The operation

A real tiki bar, not a pontoon with a roof

The company draws a line between its boats and the pontoons that have had a thatched roof added: these are purpose-built octagonal floating tiki bars, built to look the part, and they cruise at about five miles an hour. Coast Guard rules cap them at six passengers plus crew, so every cruise is effectively a small private group; larger parties book several boats and run up to 24 people together.

Every cruise carries a USCG-certified captain, Type 1 life jackets for every passenger and a first aid kit. The boat supplies a large Yeti cooler with ice and a Bluetooth Kicker speaker; guests bring their own drinks and food. Cruisin’ Tikis is a franchised brand with locations in several states; this is the Clearwater operation.

    The site does not list captains by name; reviewers mention Captains Brian and Dylan.

    Before you go

    Know before you go

    Departure

    25 Causeway Blvd, Slip 40, Clearwater Beach Marina.

    Cancellation

    Fully refundable 48 hours or more before departure. No-shows are not refunded or rescheduled.

    Weather

    The boats run in rain under the roof; the captain cancels for high wind or lightning, and you get a refund or a rain check.

    Ages and tipping

    Six and up; life jackets are required under ten. The company suggests a 20 percent gratuity for the captain.
