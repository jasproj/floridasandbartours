Fishing Charters Across Florida

Most Florida fishing charters are priced by the boat, not per person: a captain, a licensed vessel, tackle and bait for a set number of hours. The cards below show that whole-boat price and how many anglers it covers, so a four-hour inshore trip and a full-day offshore run can be compared side by side.

Inshore and backcountry trips stay in the bays, flats and mangroves on smaller boats and suit families and first-timers. Reef and wreck trips run a few miles out for snapper and grouper. Offshore and deep-sea charters go after mahi, sailfish, tuna and swordfish and usually run six to twelve hours.

Use the location filter to narrow to the Keys, Tampa Bay, the Gulf Coast or the Panhandle. Each listing opens the operator's live calendar, so the price and dates you see are what you can book.

Fishing Charter FAQs

❯ Is the price per person or for the whole boat?

Most fishing charters here are priced for the whole boat and list how many anglers that covers. A few party-boat and workshop listings are per person and are labeled that way on the card.

❯ What is included on a fishing charter?

Typically the captain, fuel, rods, tackle, bait and a fishing license for everyone aboard while on the boat. Food, drinks and gratuity are usually not included. Each listing's booking page has the operator's exact list.

❯ Inshore, offshore or backcountry — which should I pick?

Inshore and backcountry trips are calmer, shorter and better for kids and beginners. Offshore and deep-sea trips run longer, go farther and target bigger fish. If anyone in your group gets seasick, start inshore.

❯ How far ahead should I book?

Weekends and winter season in the Keys book out one to two weeks ahead. Weekday inshore trips can often be booked a few days out. The calendar on each listing shows live availability.
