15 Things to Do in St. Petersburg, Florida

Pinellas County, Florida • Updated August 2026

St. Petersburg is not the beach town most visitors picture when they think of Florida's Gulf Coast. It is a city — one with a serious arts scene, a walkable downtown, and more craft breweries per capita than just about anywhere in the state. The waterfront runs for miles, the Dali Museum draws visitors from around the world, and the mural district has turned entire city blocks into open-air galleries. This guide covers the best things to do in downtown St. Pete and the surrounding area, from museums and parks to food, beer, and the Sunshine Skyway.

St. Pete vs. St. Pete Beach: This guide covers downtown St. Petersburg — the city itself, with its museums, breweries, and urban waterfront. Looking for the barrier-island beach town? See our separate guide to things to do in St. Pete Beach.

1. The Dali Museum

The Salvador Dali Museum is the anchor of St. Petersburg's arts identity and one of the most important single-artist museums in the world. The building itself — a concrete shell cracked open by a massive geodesic glass atrium — is worth the visit before you even step inside. The permanent collection holds over 2,400 works spanning Dali's entire career, from early Impressionist experiments through the iconic melting clocks of his Surrealist period to his later nuclear mysticism paintings. The avant-garde garden wraps around the bayfront side with Dali-inspired sculptures and native plantings. Plan for at least two hours. Tickets run around $29 for adults, and Thursday evenings often feature discounted admission with live music in the garden. It sits right on the downtown waterfront, making it easy to pair with a walk along the bay.

2. St. Pete Pier and the Waterfront

The St. Pete Pier reopened in 2020 after a complete reimagining, and it has become the social center of the city. The quarter-mile pier stretches into Tampa Bay with a rooftop bar, a splash pad for kids, public art installations, fishing spots, and a small beach at the base called Spa Beach. The design is layered — you can walk the pier at water level or climb to the upper decks for panoramic views of the bay and downtown skyline. Pelicans, dolphins, and manatees are regular visitors. The surrounding waterfront park connects to Vinoy Park to the north and the Dali Museum to the south via a paved path that runs along the seawall. On weekends the area fills with joggers, families, and people just sitting on the grass watching boats pass through the bay.

3. The Downtown Mural Arts District

St. Petersburg has quietly become one of the best cities in the country for street art, largely thanks to the annual SHINE Mural Festival. Every fall, international and local artists descend on the city to paint massive murals on buildings throughout downtown. The result is hundreds of large-scale works scattered across Central Avenue, the Grand Central District, and the Warehouse Arts District. You can wander and discover them on your own, but guided mural walking tours run regularly and give context on the artists and techniques. Styles range from photorealistic portraits to abstract geometric pieces to politically charged installations. The murals rotate — some get painted over each year for new SHINE works — so the outdoor gallery is never quite the same twice. Start around the 600 block and work outward.

4. The Craft Brewery Scene

St. Pete's craft beer district is concentrated enough to walk between taprooms and diverse enough to keep things interesting all day. Green Bench Brewing anchors the scene near the heart of downtown with its open-air beer garden and rotating lineup of saisons and IPAs. Cycle Brewing focuses on barrel-aged sours and stouts that draw serious beer travelers from out of state. 3 Daughters Brewing offers a massive warehouse taproom with games, food trucks, and a family-friendly atmosphere. Cage Brewing, Pinellas Ale Works, and Overflow Brewing round out the core cluster. Most taprooms are within a few blocks of each other south of Central Avenue. The St. Pete Brew Bus runs guided crawls if you want a structured route, but honestly, the neighborhood is walkable enough that you can build your own.

5. Vinoy Park and the Waterfront Walk

Vinoy Park is the green anchor of St. Petersburg's downtown waterfront — a wide, flat stretch of lawn that wraps along the bay from the historic Vinoy Renaissance hotel north toward Coffee Pot Bayou. It hosts concerts, food festivals, and outdoor movie screenings throughout the year, but on a quiet weekday morning it is one of the most peaceful spots in the city. The waterfront walking and biking path runs from the park south past the Pier and all the way to Lassing Park in Old Southeast, covering roughly four miles of uninterrupted bayfront. You will pass sailboats moored at the yacht basin, pelicans roosting on channel markers, and the occasional dolphin surfacing in the shallows. The sunrise from Vinoy Park — facing east across Tampa Bay — is one of the best in Pinellas County.

6. Saturday Morning Market

The Saturday Morning Market runs from October through May in the Al Lang Stadium parking lot on the downtown waterfront, and it is one of the largest outdoor markets in the southeastern United States. Over 150 vendors set up each week selling local produce, artisan bread, fresh-pressed juices, handmade soaps, pottery, plants, hot prepared food, and live music. The sheer variety of the food stalls alone is worth the trip — Indian dosas, Jamaican jerk chicken, wood-fired pizza, Thai coconut bowls, and Cuban sandwiches all within a few hundred feet. Arrive before 10 a.m. to beat the crowds and get the best selection. Parking fills up fast, so consider the metered spots along Bayshore Drive or the parking garages a few blocks inland. The summer market moves to a smaller Wednesday evening format.

7. Sunken Gardens

Sunken Gardens is a four-acre botanical garden tucked into a residential neighborhood just north of downtown, and it is one of the oldest roadside attractions in Florida — dating to 1903. The garden sits in a natural sinkhole, which creates a lush, sunken microclimate sheltered from wind and road noise. Walking paths wind through tropical plantings, koi ponds, cascading waterfalls, and a butterfly garden. Flamingos — real ones — wander the grounds. It is small enough to tour in about an hour but dense enough to feel immersive, especially on a hot afternoon when the canopy provides welcome shade. Admission is around $15 for adults. The garden hosts plant sales, yoga sessions, and small events throughout the year. It pairs well with a visit to the nearby Great Explorations children's museum if you are traveling with kids.

8. Museum of Fine Arts and Imagine Museum

The Museum of Fine Arts sits on the downtown waterfront near the Vinoy and houses a collection spanning 5,000 years — Egyptian antiquities, French Impressionists, American photography, and contemporary sculpture. The building itself is bright and airy with galleries overlooking the bay. It is a quieter, more contemplative experience than the Dali and well worth a few hours. A few blocks inland, the Imagine Museum focuses entirely on contemporary American studio glass art — a niche that turns out to be far more visually stunning than it sounds. Blown glass, cast glass, and flame-worked sculptures fill two floors of a renovated building in the Edge District. If you visit one museum in St. Pete, make it the Dali. If you visit two, the MFA and Imagine Museum compete hard for that second slot.

9. Central Avenue — The Main Strip

Central Avenue is the backbone of downtown St. Pete — a long east-west corridor lined with restaurants, bars, vintage shops, galleries, and live music venues. The stretch between about 1st Street and 31st Street is the most active, with the energy shifting block by block. The east end near the waterfront leans upscale with cocktail bars and fine dining. The middle blocks around the 600s are mural-heavy and brewery-adjacent. Further west into the Grand Central District the vibe gets more eclectic — thrift stores, tattoo parlors, dive bars, and some of the best casual restaurants in the city. On weekend nights, Central Avenue fills with foot traffic moving between bars and live music spots. During the day, it is the best street in the city for just walking and browsing. The SunRunner bus rapid transit line runs the full length of Central from downtown to St. Pete Beach.

10. Sunshine Skyway Bridge

The Sunshine Skyway Bridge is one of the most dramatic drives in Florida — a cable-stayed span that rises 430 feet above Tampa Bay, connecting St. Petersburg to the Bradenton-Sarasota side of the coast. The views from the top are staggering: open Gulf waters to the west, the full sweep of Tampa Bay to the east, and the St. Pete skyline receding behind you. It is worth driving across even if you have nowhere to go on the other side. At the south end, the old bridge abutments have been converted into the Skyway Fishing Pier State Park — the longest fishing pier in the world at over a mile. Anglers pull kingfish, mackerel, and snook from the deep channel waters below. Even non-fishers come for the views and the pelican colonies. Toll is around $1.50 southbound, free northbound.

11. Fort De Soto Park

Fort De Soto Park sits on a chain of five interconnected islands at the very southern tip of Pinellas County, about 25 minutes south of downtown St. Pete. It is regularly ranked among the best beaches in the country, but it is far more than a beach. The park covers 1,136 acres and includes kayak and paddleboard launch points, a paved multi-use trail, a campground, two fishing piers, a dog beach, and the historic fort itself — a Spanish-American War-era installation with original mortar batteries you can walk through. North Beach faces Tampa Bay with calm, shallow water; East Beach faces the shipping channel with stronger currents and better shelling. Bring a kayak or rent one on-site to paddle through the mangrove trails. Fort De Soto is a full-day destination and one of the best outdoor experiences on the entire Gulf Coast.

12. St. Pete Beach Day Trip

St. Pete Beach is a separate town on a barrier island about 20 minutes west of downtown, and it deserves its own full day. The wide, white-sand Gulf beach stretches for miles, anchored at the south end by the iconic pink Don CeSar hotel — a 1928 landmark that looks like a Mediterranean palace rising out of the sand. Pass-a-Grille, at the very southern tip of the island, is a quiet, old-Florida beach village with a handful of restaurants, a small public beach, and none of the high-rise congestion found further north. The SunRunner bus connects downtown St. Pete to the beach, so you can leave the car behind. 13. The Grand Central District

Grand Central is the stretch of Central Avenue west of about 16th Street, and it has a completely different personality from the downtown core. Where downtown leans polished and tourist-ready, Grand Central is scrappier and more local — antique shops, vintage furniture stores, independent bookstores, quirky bars, and restaurants that have been around for decades. The neighborhood has a strong LGBTQ+ community presence and hosts several annual street festivals. Antique Alley is a cluster of shops between about 24th and 28th Streets that draws collectors from across the Tampa Bay region. For food, this end of Central has some hidden standouts — Jamaican, Vietnamese, Mexican, and old-school American diners that rarely show up on tourist lists. If downtown St. Pete feels like it has been discovered, Grand Central still feels like something you discovered yourself.

14. Tropicana Field Area and the Tampa Bay Rays

Tropicana Field — the domed home of the Tampa Bay Rays — sits on the western edge of downtown, and the surrounding area is in the middle of a massive transformation. The stadium itself is a relic of 1990s indoor baseball, but catching a Rays game remains one of the most affordable pro sports experiences in Florida, with upper-deck tickets often available for under $20. The real story here is what comes next: the Historic Gas Plant District redevelopment is reshaping the area around the stadium into a mixed-use neighborhood with housing, retail, green space, and cultural anchors honoring the Black community that originally occupied the site. Even now, the Tropicana Field area is worth walking through to see the scale of what is being built. Game-day energy spills into the nearby breweries and restaurants along First Avenue South.

15. The Food Scene

St. Pete's restaurant scene has matured well past the early-bird-special stereotype. The Mill is a wood-fired anchor in the Grand Central District serving rustic pizzas and house-made pastas in a converted industrial space. Bodega on Central brings Latin street food to the downtown core — Cuban sandwiches, empanadas, and cafecito that hold their own against anything in Miami. For seafood, The Oyster Bar on Fourth Street has been shucking since the 1980s, and Sea Salt on Beach Drive offers a more upscale waterfront experience. The farm-to-table movement runs strong here — Brick and Mortar, Locale Market, and FarmTable Kitchen all source from Florida farms. For a quick bite, Mazzaro's Italian Market in the northeast part of the city is a sprawling deli and bakery that feels like stepping into a market in southern Italy. You will not run out of places to eat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is St. Petersburg FL the same as St. Pete Beach?

No. St. Petersburg is a city on the west coast of Pinellas County with a downtown arts district, museums, and waterfront parks. St. Pete Beach is a separate barrier-island town about 20 minutes west, known for its wide Gulf beaches and resort hotels. Both are worth visiting, but they offer very different experiences.

How many days do you need in St. Petersburg Florida?

Two to three days is ideal for downtown St. Petersburg. That gives you time for the Dali Museum, the waterfront, the mural district, and a few meals on Central Avenue. Add a fourth day if you want to drive over the Sunshine Skyway or explore Fort De Soto Park.

What is St. Petersburg FL best known for?

St. Petersburg is best known for the Salvador Dali Museum, its vibrant street mural scene, the downtown waterfront and St. Pete Pier, a thriving craft brewery district, and year-round sunshine — the city once held the Guinness record for 768 consecutive days of sunshine.

Is downtown St. Petersburg walkable?

Yes. Downtown St. Pete is one of the most walkable areas on Florida's Gulf Coast. Central Avenue, the waterfront parks, the Dali Museum, and most breweries and restaurants are all within a roughly one-mile stretch. The SunRunner bus rapid transit line also connects downtown to St. Pete Beach if you want to leave the car behind.

When is the best time to visit St. Petersburg Florida?

October through May offers the most comfortable weather — warm days with low humidity and very little rain. The Saturday Morning Market runs October through May as well. Summer is hot and humid with afternoon thunderstorms, but hotel rates drop and crowds thin out significantly.
