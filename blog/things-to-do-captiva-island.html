10 Things to Do on Captiva Island

Lee County, Florida • Updated August 2026

Captiva Island sits at the northern tip of the Sanibel-Captiva barrier island chain, separated from the mainland by Pine Island Sound and from Sanibel by the narrow channel at Blind Pass. It is barely five miles long and a few hundred yards wide in places, but it packs a surprising amount into that sliver of sand. Quiet gulf beaches, world-class shelling, a legendary restaurant built inside a fever dream, and boat access to one of the most untouched islands in Florida. Here are ten things worth your time on Captiva.

Getting there: Captiva is reached by car via the Sanibel Causeway (toll) and then Sanibel-Captiva Road north across the Blind Pass Bridge. The drive from Fort Myers takes roughly 45 minutes. No public transit serves the island — you will need a car, bike, or golf cart rental once you arrive.

1. Captiva Beach at Turner Beach

The southern tip of Captiva, where Blind Pass separates it from Sanibel, is the island's prime beach spot. Turner Beach faces west with an unobstructed view of the Gulf of Mexico, and the tidal flow through the pass concentrates shells along the shoreline. Serious shellers arrive at low tide with headlamps before dawn, bent at the waist in what locals call the "Sanibel Stoop." By late afternoon the same stretch becomes sunset central — the sky over the gulf turns pink and gold while pelicans dive offshore. Parking is limited to a small lot on the Captiva side. Arrive early in season or plan to walk from further up Captiva Drive. There are no lifeguards, so swim with caution near the pass where currents run strong.

2. The Bubble Room

The Bubble Room is not just a restaurant — it is an experience that defies easy description. Every inch of the three-story building is covered in vintage toys, model trains, Christmas decorations, Hollywood memorabilia, and blinking lights. It has been an island institution since 1979, and the decor has only gotten more intense with each passing decade. The menu leans American comfort — steaks, seafood, prime rib — but people come for the cake. The Bubble Room's towering layer cakes are legendary: the Orange Crunch Cake alone could anchor a weekend trip. Expect a wait during peak season, especially on weekends without a reservation. Go for lunch on a weekday if you want a shorter line. The place is kid-friendly in the best possible way.

3. Sunset Sailing Cruises from South Seas

Captiva's position on the Gulf coast makes it one of the best places in Florida to watch the sun drop below the water, and watching it from a sailboat is the upgrade worth paying for. Several operators run sunset cruises from the marina at South Seas Island Resort, typically departing ninety minutes before sundown. You will sail past the tip of North Captiva, through Pine Island Sound, and into open gulf water as the light shifts. Most cruises include drinks and light snacks. Some run catamaran trips with larger groups; others offer private charters for a quieter experience. Dolphins frequently ride the bow wake, and in winter months you may spot manatees in the shallows near the marina. Book at least a day ahead during high season — these fill up fast.

4. Kayaking and Paddleboarding Pine Island Sound

The back side of Captiva — the bay side, facing Pine Island Sound — is a paddler's paradise. The water is shallow, warm, and sheltered from gulf swells, with mangrove islands and oyster bars breaking up the expanse. Kayak and paddleboard rentals are available at multiple spots along Captiva Drive and through the South Seas marina. Morning paddles offer the calmest water and the best chance at wildlife sightings: bottlenose dolphins feed in the channels, manatees drift through the seagrass beds, and osprey nest in the mangroves. Guided eco-tours are available for those who want narration and a route planned around tidal conditions. The paddle from Captiva to Buck Key, a small mangrove island just offshore, is a popular two-hour loop that works for beginners.

5. Cayo Costa State Park Day Trip

Cayo Costa is a barrier island just north of Captiva, accessible only by boat, and it feels like stepping back a hundred years. Nine miles of undeveloped gulf beach, no cars, no restaurants, and some of the best shelling in all of Florida. Ferry services run daily from Captiva's marinas — the ride takes about 30 minutes. Bring everything you need: water, food, sunscreen, and a bag for shells. The beach faces the open gulf and the sand is soft and pale. A trail system cuts through the interior pine forest to the bay side, where you can wade the flats. Primitive cabins and tent camping are available for those who want to stay overnight. For most visitors, a half-day trip is enough time to walk the beach, fill a shell bag, and swim in water that feels private even in February.

6. Fishing Charters from McCarthy's Marina

McCarthy's Marina sits on the bay side of Captiva and has been the island's fishing hub for decades. Charter captains here know Pine Island Sound, the passes, and the nearshore gulf reefs the way taxi drivers know side streets. Inshore trips target snook, redfish, and spotted seatrout in the mangrove creeks and grass flats. Offshore charters head into the gulf for grouper, snapper, kingfish, and — in summer — tarpon, the silver kings that cruise the passes. Half-day trips work well for families and casual anglers. Full-day offshore runs are better suited for experienced fishers who want to reach deeper structure. Most charters provide rods, tackle, bait, and licenses. Bring polarized sunglasses, a hat, and plenty of water. Book early in tarpon season — the best captains fill their calendars months ahead.

7. South Seas Island Resort

South Seas occupies the entire northern third of Captiva Island — a 330-acre resort compound with its own marina, golf course, pools, tennis courts, restaurants, and a stretch of private beach. Even if you are not staying there, the resort's marina is the departure point for many boat tours, fishing charters, and ferry services. Guests have access to kayak and bike rentals, a nature center, and kids' programs. The property was heavily rebuilt after Hurricane Ian in 2022 and reopened with updated facilities while keeping its old-Florida resort character. It functions almost as a self-contained village: you could spend an entire week without leaving the grounds. For day visitors, the marina area and its restaurants are the main draw, along with booking tours that launch from the docks.

8. Bike Sanibel-Captiva Road Through Ding Darling

One of the best bike rides in Southwest Florida starts on Captiva and runs south along Sanibel-Captiva Road through the J.N. "Ding" Darling National Wildlife Refuge. The shared-use path is flat, paved, and shaded by a canopy of sea grapes and gumbo-limbo trees. The stretch through the refuge passes mangrove wetlands where roseate spoonbills, great blue herons, and white ibis feed along the shore. The full ride from mid-Captiva to the eastern end of Sanibel and back is roughly 20 miles round trip — a manageable morning ride for casual cyclists. Bike rentals are available at several shops on both islands. Time the ride for early morning when the heat is lower, traffic is light, and the birds are most active. For more on what to do once you reach the other end, see our guide to things to do on Sanibel Island.

9. Chapel by the Sea and Island History

The Captiva Chapel by the Sea is a small, white-frame church set among palm trees near the center of the island. Built in 1903, it is one of the oldest structures on Captiva and holds nondenominational services from November through April. The chapel and its surrounding cemetery tell the compressed history of a barrier island: fishing families, pioneers, hurricanes that redrew the coastline, and the slow transformation from isolated outpost to resort destination. The Captiva Island Historical Society maintains records and occasional exhibits about the island's past, including its pre-development days when it was accessible only by boat. A walk through the chapel grounds is quiet and free — a welcome contrast to the beach crowds. It takes fifteen minutes and offers a different perspective on the island beneath the vacation veneer.

10. Tween Waters Island Resort and Andy Rosse Lane

Tween Waters Island Resort sits at the narrowest point of Captiva, where the gulf beach and the bay shore are separated by just a few hundred feet — hence the name. The resort has been an island anchor since the 1930s and was a favorite retreat of cartoonist Jay Norwood "Ding" Darling, the conservationist who gave his name to the Sanibel refuge. Today the property offers lodging, a pool, a marina, and the Crow's Nest lounge, which hosts live music and is one of the few nightlife options on the island. Just south of Tween Waters, Andy Rosse Lane is Captiva's version of a main street: a short block lined with shops, galleries, ice cream stands, and restaurants. It is walkable in five minutes but worth lingering over when the pace of the island catches up with you.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you get to Captiva Island?

Captiva is connected to Sanibel Island by the Blind Pass Bridge. From Fort Myers, take the Sanibel Causeway (cash or SunPass toll) onto Sanibel, then follow Sanibel-Captiva Road north. The drive from Fort Myers takes about 45 minutes. The nearest commercial airport is Southwest Florida International (RSW), about 30 miles away. There is no public transit to the island — you will need a car, bike, or golf cart rental.

What is the best time of year to visit Captiva Island?

Peak season runs from December through April. The weather is warm and dry, humidity is manageable, and winter cold fronts push shells onto the beaches for prime shelling. Summer is hotter and wetter but brings lower prices and fewer crowds. Hurricane season runs June through November, so monitor forecasts if you are visiting during those months.

Is Captiva Island the same as Sanibel Island?

No. Captiva is a separate barrier island immediately north of Sanibel, connected by a short bridge at Blind Pass. Captiva is smaller and more resort-oriented, with a quieter, more secluded feel. Sanibel has more shops, restaurants, and the Ding Darling refuge. Most visitors explore both islands in the same trip — our Sanibel guide covers the highlights.

Can you do a day trip to Captiva Island?

Absolutely. Many visitors stay in Fort Myers or on Sanibel and drive to Captiva for the day. You can hit Captiva Beach, grab lunch at the Bubble Room, and catch a sunset from Andy Rosse Lane — all in one afternoon. For a longer day, add a boat trip to Cayo Costa or a kayak paddle through Pine Island Sound.
