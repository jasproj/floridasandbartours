10 Things to Do in Madeira Beach

Pinellas County, Florida • Updated August 2026

Madeira Beach sits on a narrow barrier island between the Gulf of Mexico and Boca Ciega Bay, just south of Clearwater and a short bridge from Treasure Island. Locals call it "Mad Beach," and the nickname fits — this compact stretch of sand packs in more waterfront restaurants, fishing charters, and on-the-water experiences than towns twice its size. Johns Pass Village anchors the whole scene, but there's plenty beyond the boardwalk. Here are ten things worth your time.

Getting oriented: Madeira Beach is on Gulf Boulevard (the main road running the length of the barrier islands) in Pinellas County. Johns Pass Village sits at the southern tip where the bridge crosses to Treasure Island. Free and metered parking is available along Gulf Boulevard and in the Johns Pass lots — arrive before 10 a.m. on weekends or plan to circle.

1. Johns Pass Village & Boardwalk

Johns Pass Village is the commercial heart of Madeira Beach and one of the most-visited waterfront districts on Florida's Gulf Coast. Built along the channel that separates Madeira Beach from Treasure Island, the boardwalk runs roughly a quarter mile and is lined with over a hundred shops, seafood restaurants, and tour operators. You'll find everything from shell shops and surf gear to fresh-catch markets and waterfront tiki bars. The fishing fleet docks right at the boardwalk, so there's always something to watch — charter boats heading out at dawn, pelicans working the pilings, dolphins cruising the channel at dusk. It's free to walk, easy to spend a full afternoon, and the best starting point for first-time visitors.

2. Deep-Sea Fishing Charters from Johns Pass

Johns Pass is home to one of the largest fleets of fishing charter boats in the southeastern United States. Half-day trips head into the Gulf targeting grouper, snapper, kingfish, and amberjack, while full-day and overnight trips push further offshore for pelagic species like mahi-mahi and cobia. Party boats offer affordable shared trips if you don't want to book a private charter — most provide rods, bait, tackle, and a fishing license. The pass itself is a productive fishing spot, and you'll see anglers casting from the bridge and the jetties at any hour. If deep-sea isn't your speed, inshore guides work the flats of Boca Ciega Bay for redfish, snook, and spotted seatrout on light tackle.

3. Madeira Beach and Archibald Beach Park

The beach itself deserves a dedicated stop, not just a drive-by on the way to Johns Pass. Archibald Beach Park is the main public access point, with free parking (limited), restrooms, showers, and a paved path to the sand. The beach here is wide, the sand is fine and white, and the Gulf water stays warm enough for swimming from April through November. Unlike some of the more crowded Pinellas beaches, Madeira Beach tends to have breathing room — especially if you walk a few blocks north of the park entrance. Bring a cooler, set up chairs, and plan to stay. The sunsets from this stretch are some of the best on the Gulf Coast, with unobstructed views straight west over open water.

4. The Grouper Sandwich Scene

Madeira Beach takes its grouper seriously. The blackened grouper sandwich is practically the town's unofficial dish, and several spots compete for the title of best on the beach. Mad Beach Fish House serves theirs on a brioche bun with a slaw that locals swear by. Sculley's Johns Pass Boardwalk Grille does a fried version that's been pulling crowds for years. The Boardwalk Grill and Dockside Dave's are both strong contenders. Most places source their grouper from the fleet docked steps away, so freshness isn't a question — it's a given. Order it blackened, fried, or grilled; pair it with a cold beer and a water view. If you visit Madeira Beach and skip the grouper sandwich, you missed the point.

5. Dolphin Tours and Sunset Cruises from Johns Pass

Bottlenose dolphins are year-round residents of the waters around Johns Pass, and spotting them is close to guaranteed on any boat outing. Several operators run dedicated dolphin-watching cruises that motor through the Intracoastal Waterway and into the Gulf, with narration on local wildlife and ecology. Sunset cruises are the other staple — most depart around ninety minutes before sundown and loop through the pass and along the beachfront while the sky does its thing. Some boats are party-style with music and a bar; others are quieter pontoon setups better suited for families. Either way, being on the water at golden hour in this part of Florida is hard to beat. Book a day ahead during peak season.

6. Paddleboarding and Kayaking on Boca Ciega Bay

The bay side of Madeira Beach is where the paddleboard and kayak crowd goes. Boca Ciega Bay is shallow, sheltered from Gulf swells, and full of wildlife — manatees in winter, dolphins year-round, herons and ospreys overhead. Several rental shops near Johns Pass will set you up with a board or kayak by the hour, and guided eco-tours are available if you want someone to point out the mangrove channels and seagrass flats worth exploring. Early morning is the best window: flat water, fewer boats, and better odds of spotting marine life. It's also a solid workout. If you've never tried stand-up paddleboarding, the calm bay side of the island is one of the best places in Tampa Bay to learn.

7. Shell Key Preserve

Shell Key Preserve is a 1,800-acre barrier island and aquatic preserve south of Pass-a-Grille, accessible only by boat. From Madeira Beach, you can reach it on a guided boat tour, a rented kayak, or a sandbar excursion that includes a Shell Key stop. The island is undeveloped — no buildings, no boardwalks, no concessions — just white sand, shallow turquoise water, and some of the best shelling on the Gulf Coast. Whelks, fighting conchs, sand dollars, and lightning whelks wash up regularly. The preserve is also a nesting ground for shorebirds, so some sections are roped off during nesting season (spring through summer). Bring water, sunscreen, and a mesh bag for shells. There's no shade.

8. Walk or Bike to Treasure Island and Redington Beach

Madeira Beach connects directly to Treasure Island via the Johns Pass bridge, and Redington Beach borders it to the north. Gulf Boulevard runs the full length of the barrier islands, and walking or biking between towns is one of the best ways to see this stretch of coast. The sidewalk along Gulf Boulevard is flat and continuous, and a round trip from Johns Pass to Sunset Beach on Treasure Island is about three miles. Heading north, Redington Beach is quieter and more residential — a nice change of pace. Rent a bike from one of the shops near Johns Pass, or just walk the beach at low tide and cover as much ground as the sand allows. 9. The Snack Shack and Beach Bar Culture

Madeira Beach has a strong beach-bar identity. The Snack Shack, tucked right off the sand near Archibald Beach Park, is a local favorite for no-frills fish tacos, frozen drinks, and cold beer with your feet still sandy. Along Johns Pass, tiki bars like Hooters and the Boardwalk Beach Bar keep the drinks flowing with live music on weekends. The vibe across all of them is the same: casual, loud, salt air, and nobody's checking what you're wearing. This isn't a bottle-service beach town. If you're coming from Clearwater Beach, Madeira's bar scene feels more local and less resort-driven. Pull up a stool, order a rum runner, and watch the boats come in.

10. John's Pass Seafood Festival and Events

The John's Pass Seafood Festival is the biggest annual event in Madeira Beach, held every year over a long weekend in late October. It draws over 100,000 visitors and features dozens of seafood vendors, live music stages, an arts and crafts market, and boat displays along the boardwalk. The festival is free to attend, and the food is the main draw — fried grouper, stone crab claws, smoked fish dip, shrimp baskets, and everything in between. Beyond the festival, Johns Pass hosts smaller events throughout the year: boat parades during the holidays, live music series in spring, and fishing tournaments that bring the competitive angling crowd to town. Check the Madeira Beach events calendar before your trip — there's usually something going on.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Madeira Beach worth visiting?

Absolutely. Madeira Beach combines a laid-back Gulf Coast vibe with Johns Pass Village, one of the busiest waterfront boardwalks on Florida's west coast. You get wide sandy beaches, world-class grouper sandwiches, dolphin cruises, deep-sea fishing, and easy access to neighboring Treasure Island and Clearwater Beach — all without the spring-break crowds.

What is Johns Pass Village known for?

Johns Pass Village is a waterfront boardwalk district on the channel between Madeira Beach and Treasure Island. It's known for its mix of shops, seafood restaurants, charter fishing boats, dolphin tours, jet ski rentals, and sunset cruises. It's the departure point for most on-the-water activities in the area and draws over four million visitors per year.

How do I get to Shell Key Preserve from Madeira Beach?

Shell Key Preserve is a boat-access-only barrier island south of Pass-a-Grille. From Madeira Beach, you can reach it by booking a guided boat tour from Johns Pass, renting a kayak or paddleboard for the crossing from Boca Ciega Bay, or joining a sandbar and island-hopping excursion that includes a Shell Key stop. There are no ferries or public transit routes to the island.

When is the best time to visit Madeira Beach?

October through April offers the most comfortable weather — warm days, low humidity, and minimal rain. Summer is hotter and wetter but great for fishing and water sports, and hotel rates drop significantly. The John's Pass Seafood Festival in late October is one of the biggest draws of the year if you can time your trip around it.
