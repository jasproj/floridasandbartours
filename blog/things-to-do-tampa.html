15 Things to Do in Tampa

Hillsborough County, Florida • Updated August 2026

Tampa is the anchor of the Gulf Coast — a real city with a waterfront downtown, a historic district with actual history, and a food scene that's been shaped by Cuban, Spanish, and Italian immigrants for over a century. It's not a beach town (the beaches are 30 minutes across the bay), but it's the reason the beaches have an airport, restaurants worth the drive, and something to do when it rains.

Here's what's worth doing — from the Riverwalk and Ybor City to the boat tours and day trips that start from Tampa Bay.

Getting oriented: Downtown Tampa sits on the east shore of Tampa Bay. Ybor City is northeast of downtown. The beaches (Clearwater, St. Pete Beach, Indian Rocks) are 30-40 minutes west across the bay. Tampa International Airport (TPA) is 15 minutes from downtown — one of the best-rated airports in the US.

1. Tampa Riverwalk

The Tampa Riverwalk is a 2.6-mile paved path along the Hillsborough River connecting downtown's major attractions — from the Tampa Convention Center through Curtis Hixon Waterfront Park past the Tampa Museum of Art, Glazer Children's Museum, the Straz Center, Armature Works, and up to Water Works Park. It's the spine of downtown Tampa. Walk it, bike it (Coast Bike Share stations along the route), or take the Pirate Water Taxi. The stretch between Curtis Hixon Park and Armature Works is the most scenic. On weekends, the parks along the Riverwalk host food trucks, fitness classes, and live music.

2. Ybor City

Ybor City was the cigar capital of the world in the early 1900s — a neighborhood built by Cuban, Spanish, Italian, and German immigrants who rolled cigars by hand in massive factories. The brick streets, wrought-iron balconies, and factory buildings are still there. Seventh Avenue (La Septima) is the main strip: restaurants, bars, cigar shops, and the Columbia Restaurant (Florida's oldest, opened 1905 — the 1905 Salad and the Cuban sandwich are the orders). By day, it's a historic district with a great state museum (Ybor City Museum State Park). By night, it's Tampa's nightlife hub. The hand-rolled cigar shops (Tabanero, J.C. Newman) are worth a stop whether or not you smoke.

3. Busch Gardens Tampa Bay

Busch Gardens is a theme park built around an African wildlife theme — roller coasters, animal exhibits, and shows on a 335-acre campus. The coasters are the main draw: SheiKra (dive coaster, 200-foot vertical drop), Cheetah Hunt (triple launch), Montu (inverted), Kumba (classic), and Iron Gwazi (hybrid wood-steel, consistently ranked among the world's best). The Serengeti Safari section has giraffes, zebras, elephants, and rhinos in open habitats. It's a full-day park. If you're comparing it to the Orlando parks — Busch Gardens has better coasters and shorter lines, at a lower price.

4. Cuban Sandwich Trail

Tampa claims the Cuban sandwich — not Miami, not Havana, Tampa. The original Tampa Cuban uses Genoa salami (Miami's doesn't), pressed on Cuban bread from La Segunda Central Bakery (operating since 1915 and still the standard). The best-known spots: Columbia Restaurant (Ybor City), Brocato's Sandwich Shop (a counter with a line), West Tampa Sandwich Shop, La Teresita, and Aguila Sandwich Shop. Tampa's city council literally passed a resolution declaring the Cuban sandwich Tampa's signature. Whether you agree with the politics or not, the sandwich is the real thing, and La Segunda's bread is the foundation.

5. The Florida Aquarium

A modern aquarium on the Channelside waterfront in downtown Tampa. The exhibits trace a drop of water from a Florida spring through wetlands, beaches, coral reefs, and the open ocean. The coral reef exhibit is the centerpiece — a 500,000-gallon tank with sharks, rays, sea turtles, and schooling fish. There's a touch tank, a splash pad for kids, and behind-the-scenes penguin and shark encounters (extra fee). The Wild Dolphin Cruise departs from the aquarium's dock — a 75-minute boat ride into Tampa Bay to see dolphins in the wild. Allow three to four hours.

6. Armature Works

A 1910 streetcar maintenance building on the Hillsborough River, converted into a food hall and event space. The Heights Public Market inside has a rotating roster of food vendors — poke, barbecue, ramen, tacos, coffee, bakery, juice bar. The outdoor patio on the river is one of the best places to eat in Tampa at sunset. Upstairs is a cocktail bar (M. Bird) with a rooftop terrace overlooking downtown and the river. It anchors the Tampa Heights neighborhood, which has become one of the city's most interesting food and drink corridors. The Pirate Water Taxi stops here.

7. Bayshore Boulevard

Bayshore Boulevard runs along the west shore of Hillsborough Bay — a 4.5-mile waterfront sidewalk that's the longest continuous sidewalk in the world. Runners, cyclists, and walkers use it daily. The views across the bay toward Davis Islands and downtown are good, and the historic homes along the route (Hyde Park and Palma Ceia neighborhoods) are some of Tampa's finest. The stretch from Gandy Boulevard to downtown is the most scenic. If you run or walk in the morning, you'll see dolphins in the bay.

8. Tampa Bay Boat Tours

Tampa Bay is a massive estuary — the largest open-water estuary in Florida — and the boat tours that run from downtown and the surrounding marinas are a different experience from the Gulf Coast beach cruises. Dolphin-watching tours run from the Florida Aquarium dock and the Channelside area. Sandbar tours head to Beer Can Island (a shallow sandbar in the bay where boats anchor and people wade). Sunset cruises loop under the Gandy Bridge and out into the bay. The Pirate Water Taxi runs a hop-on, hop-off route along the Riverwalk and out to Sparkman Wharf.

9. Hyde Park Village

Hyde Park is Tampa's upscale neighborhood just south of downtown — tree-lined streets, historic bungalows, and a walkable village of shops and restaurants around a central square. The restaurant scene here has evolved beyond the old-money steakhouse stereotype: Bern's Steak House (still iconic — the dessert room upstairs is a Tampa legend), Timpano, Haven, and On Swann are all here or nearby. Hyde Park Village itself is a shopping district with boutiques, galleries, and sidewalk cafes. SoHo (South Howard Avenue) connects Hyde Park to the bar and restaurant strip that runs south toward Bayshore.

10. Sparkman Wharf

Sparkman Wharf replaced the old Channelside complex on the downtown waterfront — an outdoor dining and entertainment district with shipping-container restaurants arranged around a central lawn facing the bay. The food lineup rotates but typically includes tacos, burgers, sushi, pizza, and craft beer. There's a beer garden (Fermented Reality) and live music on weekends. It's also the embarkation point for cruise ships, so the energy changes depending on whether there's a ship in port. The view of the bay and the cruise terminal at sunset is solid.

11. Craft Brewery Scene

Tampa has one of the best craft brewery scenes in the Southeast. Cigar City Brewing (Jai Alai IPA, Hunahpu's Imperial Stout) is the anchor — their taproom in the Spruce Street area has a full lineup on tap. Coppertail Brewing is in a massive Ybor-adjacent warehouse with food trucks. Angry Chair (barrel-aged stouts) in Seminole Heights. Hidden Springs Ale Works. Magnanimous Brewing in the Tampa Heights corridor. The Seminole Heights neighborhood (north of downtown) has become a brewery and restaurant district — Angry Chair, Hidden Springs, The Independent, Ichicoro Ramen, and Rooster & the Till are all within walking distance.

12. Big Cat Rescue

Big Cat Rescue is a 67-acre sanctuary in Citrus Park (north Tampa) that houses rescued big cats — tigers, lions, leopards, cougars, bobcats, and other species removed from roadside zoos, private ownership, and the exotic pet trade. It gained national attention from the Netflix documentary Tiger King. Guided tours run daily (reservation required) and walk you through the sanctuary on elevated walkways overlooking the enclosures. The cats are rescues, not performers — the tours are educational, not entertainment. Night tours and feeding tours are available for additional cost.

13. Lettuce Lake Conservation Park

A 240-acre park on the Hillsborough River about 15 minutes north of downtown. The boardwalk trail through the cypress swamp and hardwood hammock is one of the best nature walks near any Florida city — alligators, river otters, turtles, pileated woodpeckers, and barred owls are regularly spotted. There's a kayak and canoe launch, a playground, and a 1.2-mile paved exercise trail. The observation tower at the end of the boardwalk overlooks the river. It's a reminder that Tampa sits at the edge of genuine Florida wild, not just strip malls and highways.

14. Day Trip: Clearwater Beach and St. Pete Beach

Tampa doesn't have Gulf beaches, but the best Gulf Coast beaches are 30 to 40 minutes west. Clearwater Beach is the most famous — white sand, Pier 60 sunset celebration, dolphin cruises, and the Clearwater Marine Aquarium. St. Pete Beach is quieter, with the Don CeSar hotel and Pass-a-Grille's old-Florida vibe. Indian Rocks Beach and Treasure Island are smaller and less crowded. The Courtney Campbell Causeway to Clearwater and the Howard Frankland Bridge to St. Pete are the two routes across Tampa Bay — both have water views the whole way.

15. Gasparilla and the Events Calendar

Gasparilla is Tampa's signature event — a pirate-themed festival held every late January/early February where a full-size pirate ship invades Bayshore Boulevard and the city throws a massive parade and street party. It's Tampa's Mardi Gras. Beyond Gasparilla, Tampa's events calendar includes the Florida State Fair (February), Gasparilla Music Festival (March), the Guavaween Halloween festival in Ybor City, and spring training baseball at Steinbrenner Field (Yankees) and nearby Clearwater (Phillies) and Dunedin (Blue Jays). The Lightning (hockey) and Bucs (football) games at Amalie Arena and Raymond James Stadium are major draws in their respective seasons.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Tampa worth visiting?

Yes. Tampa has more depth than most Florida beach destinations — real history in Ybor City, a walkable waterfront downtown, a food scene anchored by Cuban and Latin traditions, Busch Gardens' coasters, and the Gulf Coast beaches 30 minutes away. It's a city, not just a resort.

What is Tampa known for?

Ybor City, Cuban sandwiches, Busch Gardens, the Tampa Riverwalk, craft breweries (especially Cigar City), the Tampa Bay Buccaneers and Lightning, and its role as the metro anchor of the Gulf Coast beach region.

When is the best time to visit Tampa?

October through April for warm weather without the intense summer humidity. February and March are ideal — post-Gasparilla, spring training baseball, and perfect weather. Summer is hot and humid with afternoon thunderstorms, but theme park and hotel prices drop.

Does Tampa have beaches?

Tampa sits on the bay, not the Gulf. The major Gulf beaches — Clearwater Beach, St. Pete Beach, Indian Rocks Beach — are 30-40 minutes west. Ben T. Davis Beach on the Courtney Campbell Causeway is the closest thing Tampa proper has to a beach.

How far is Tampa from Orlando?

About 85 miles, roughly 1.5 hours via I-4. Tampa International Airport (TPA) is 15 minutes from downtown and consistently rated one of the best airports in the US.
