12 Things to Do in Indian Rocks Beach

Pinellas County, Florida • Updated August 2026

Indian Rocks Beach sits on a narrow barrier island along Florida's Gulf Coast, sandwiched between the Intracoastal Waterway and the Gulf of Mexico. Unlike its flashier neighbors to the north and south, IRB has no high-rise hotels, no spring-break scene, and no boardwalk crammed with souvenir shops. What it does have is a mile-and-a-half stretch of white-sand beach, a tight-knit local community, and the kind of quiet that people come back for year after year.

Getting oriented: Indian Rocks Beach is in Pinellas County, about 25 minutes west of Tampa and 15 minutes south of Clearwater Beach. Gulf Boulevard runs the length of the island. The town is small enough to bike or walk, and most restaurants, bars, and beach access points are along or just off Gulf Blvd. The Intracoastal Waterway runs along the eastern shore — that is your kayaking, paddleboarding, and dolphin-watching side.

1. Spend a Day on Indian Rocks Beach

The beach itself is the main attraction, and for good reason. Indian Rocks Beach has the same sugar-white sand and clear turquoise Gulf water you will find at Clearwater Beach, but without the wall of condos and the fight for parking. The shoreline is backed by modest beach houses and low-slung motels, giving it a distinctly old-Florida feel that has become increasingly rare on this coast. The sand is wide enough to spread out even on busy weekends, and the Gulf here is shallow and calm — perfect for wading with kids or floating on a raft. Bring a chair, an umbrella, and a cooler. You will not need much else.

2. Watch the Sunset from the Gulf

Sunset is a daily event in Indian Rocks Beach, and locals treat it like one. People gather along the waterline and on the low seawalls that line parts of Gulf Boulevard starting about 30 minutes before the sun drops. The western horizon over the Gulf is wide open with no islands or structures in the way, so you get the full show — from the first orange glow to the moment the sun slips below the water. Some evenings you will catch a green flash if conditions are right. The stretch of beach near the 27th Avenue access is a popular sunset spot, and Crabby Bill's deck gives you the same view with a drink in hand. No reservation needed. Just show up and face west.

3. Explore Kolb Park and the IRB Nature Preserve

Kolb Park sits on the Intracoastal side of Indian Rocks Beach and offers a different pace from the Gulf-facing sand. The park has a small but well-maintained nature trail that winds through coastal scrub and mangroves, with interpretive signs about the local ecosystem. Wading birds — herons, egrets, and the occasional roseate spoonbill — are common along the water's edge, especially in the early morning. There is a playground, a picnic pavilion, and a kayak launch point that puts you directly on the Intracoastal. It is a good spot to stretch your legs if you have been beach-sitting all day, and it gives you a feel for the quieter, wilder side of barrier island life that most visitors never see.

4. Eat Waterfront Seafood at Crabby Bill's

Crabby Bill's is the landmark restaurant in Indian Rocks Beach, and it has been here since 1983. It sits directly on the Gulf with an open-air deck where you can watch the waves while cracking stone crab claws or working through a pile of steamed shrimp. The menu is straightforward Florida seafood — grouper sandwiches, fried clam strips, conch fritters, and cold beer. It is not fine dining and it does not pretend to be. The wait can be long on weekends, especially near sunset, so plan accordingly or grab a spot at the bar. Guppy's on the Beach, a few blocks north, offers a more upscale take on Gulf seafood with the same water views. Both are walkable from most IRB accommodations.

5. Paddleboard or Kayak the Intracoastal Waterway

The Intracoastal Waterway on the east side of Indian Rocks Beach is flat, protected, and perfect for paddling. Several outfitters along Gulf Boulevard and the nearby marinas rent stand-up paddleboards and kayaks by the hour. The water here is calmer than the Gulf, and you can paddle north or south along the mangrove-lined shore looking for dolphins, manatees, and rays. Early morning is the best time — the water is glassy, the boat traffic is minimal, and the wildlife is active. Guided kayak tours are available if you want someone to point out the birds and steer you to the best channels. It is one of the most peaceful things you can do in Indian Rocks Beach, and a welcome change from lying on the sand.

6. Go Fishing — Pier, Wading, or Charter

Fishing is woven into the culture of Indian Rocks Beach. Wade fishermen work the shallow Gulf flats in the early morning targeting snook, trout, and redfish. The Intracoastal side produces sheepshead, mangrove snapper, and the occasional tarpon during summer months. If you want to get offshore, charter boats depart from nearby marinas in Indian Shores and Madeira Beach for half-day and full-day trips targeting grouper, kingfish, and amberjack. The Redington Long Pier, a short drive south, is open to the public for a small fee and is a solid spot for casual fishing with kids. Bait shops along Gulf Boulevard can set you up with tackle, a license, and advice on what is biting.

7. Day Trip to Clearwater Beach

Clearwater Beach is only 15 minutes north of Indian Rocks Beach, and the contrast is striking. Where IRB is quiet and residential, Clearwater is a full-blown beach destination with a busy boardwalk, Pier 60 sunset celebrations, and a strip of restaurants and shops along Mandalay Avenue. It is worth a half-day visit to walk the pier, catch a street performer, or rent a jet ski. The sand at Clearwater is consistently ranked among the best in the country, and the water is the same warm, clear Gulf you get at IRB. Go during the week to avoid the worst crowds, and head back to Indian Rocks Beach when you want your quiet back. For the full breakdown, see our guide to things to do in Clearwater Beach.

8. Look for Sea Turtles at the Nature Preserve

Indian Rocks Beach is an active sea turtle nesting area from May through October. Loggerhead turtles are the most common species, and they crawl up the beach at night to lay eggs in the sand. The nests are staked and roped off by local volunteers, and you will see them dotted along the shoreline during nesting season. The Indian Rocks Beach Nature Preserve, a small protected parcel on the Gulf side, is one of the best spots to learn about the nesting program — look for the interpretive signage near the dune crossovers. If you are on the beach after dark during nesting season, keep lights off and give any turtles you encounter a wide berth. The hatchlings emerge roughly 60 days after nesting and make their way to the water by moonlight.

9. Grab Craft Beer and Hit the Local Bars

Indian Rocks Beach has a low-key bar scene that caters to locals and in-the-know visitors rather than tourists. Keegan's Seafood Grille is a neighborhood favorite with cold drinks, a solid raw bar, and an outdoor deck that fills up on weekend afternoons. Slyce Pizza Bar serves wood-fired pizza and a rotating tap list in a casual setting that works for families and late-night crowds alike. JD's Restaurant and Lounge is old-school IRB — a dimly lit locals' bar with strong pours and no pretense. If you want to branch out, the craft brewery scene in nearby Largo and Seminole is growing fast, with several taprooms within a 15-minute drive. But most nights in IRB, a cold beer on a patio with a Gulf breeze is all you need.

10. Find the Hidden Beach Access Points

One of the best-kept secrets about Indian Rocks Beach is its network of small, numbered public beach access points scattered along Gulf Boulevard. There are more than two dozen of them, and most are nothing more than a narrow path between two houses leading to the sand. They are marked with small blue signs that are easy to miss if you are driving. The accesses near 1st Avenue and 3rd Avenue have small parking areas and are the most well-known. But the ones farther north — around 17th and 20th Avenues — tend to be emptier and feel almost private. If you are staying in a rental without direct beach access, these paths are how you get to the water. Walk a few blocks in either direction from any of them and you will likely have a wide stretch of sand nearly to yourself.

11. Day Trip to St. Pete Beach

St. Pete Beach is about 20 minutes south of Indian Rocks Beach and offers a different Gulf Coast experience. The historic Don CeSar hotel — a massive pink Mediterranean Revival landmark — anchors the south end of the beach and is worth seeing even if you are not staying there. Pass-a-Grille, at the very southern tip, is a quiet village with excellent shelling, a handful of restaurants, and a stretch of beach that faces both the Gulf and Tampa Bay. The Corey Avenue shopping district has local boutiques and a weekend market. St. Pete Beach is bigger and busier than IRB but still more relaxed than Clearwater. Check out our guide to things to do in St. Pete Beach for the full list.

12. Visit Largo Central Park, Florida Botanical Gardens, and Heritage Village

When you need a break from the beach, head east to Largo — it is a 10-minute drive from Indian Rocks Beach. Largo Central Park is a 70-acre green space with walking trails, playgrounds, and a recreation complex. Right next to it, the Florida Botanical Gardens covers 30 acres of curated gardens — the butterfly garden and the native plant trail are the highlights, and admission is free. Heritage Village, also adjacent, is an open-air museum with restored historical buildings from across Pinellas County, including a schoolhouse, a railroad depot, and a citrus packing house. Together, these three attractions fill an easy half-day and give you a look at what life was like on this coast before the condos and the tourist trade arrived.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Indian Rocks Beach a good alternative to Clearwater Beach?

Yes. Indian Rocks Beach has the same white sand and clear Gulf water as Clearwater Beach but with a fraction of the crowds. There are no high-rise hotels lining the shore, and the atmosphere is residential and relaxed rather than resort-driven. IRB is especially popular with families and repeat visitors who want a quiet beach day without the congestion of Clearwater's main strip. It is only 15 minutes south, so you can easily visit both in the same trip.

Is there free parking at Indian Rocks Beach?

Free street parking exists along Gulf Boulevard and some residential side streets, but it is limited and fills up quickly on weekends and holidays. The public beach accesses at 1st and 3rd Avenues have small parking areas. Several access points have metered spaces. Your best bet for free parking is to arrive before 10 AM on weekends, or visit on a weekday when competition for spots drops significantly. If you are staying nearby, biking or walking to the beach is the easiest option.

What is the best time of year to visit Indian Rocks Beach?

October through May is the most comfortable stretch — lower humidity, warm but not oppressive temperatures, and very little rain. Water temperatures are pleasant from April through November. The summer months (June through September) are hot and humid with daily afternoon thunderstorms, but the Gulf water is at its warmest and the crowds thin out compared to spring. Sea turtle nesting season runs from May through October, which adds a unique wildlife dimension to a summer visit.

Are there good restaurants within walking distance of the beach?

Indian Rocks Beach is compact enough that most restaurants are walkable from the main beach areas. Crabby Bill's sits directly on the Gulf and serves classic Florida seafood. Guppy's on the Beach is a few blocks north with a slightly more upscale menu. Keegan's Seafood Grille, Slyce Pizza Bar, and JD's are all along or near Gulf Boulevard. You can eat well for a week in IRB without getting in a car.

Can you see dolphins from Indian Rocks Beach?

Dolphins are spotted regularly from the shore, particularly in the early morning and late afternoon when they feed close to the beach. The Intracoastal Waterway side is another reliable area — paddlers and kayakers frequently encounter pods working the shallows. For a dedicated dolphin experience, guided boat tours and kayak eco-tours depart from marinas in Indian Shores and Clearwater, most of them within a short drive of IRB.
