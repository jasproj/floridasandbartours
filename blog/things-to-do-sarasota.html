15 Things to Do in Sarasota

Sarasota County, Florida • Updated August 2026

Sarasota sits on the Gulf Coast about an hour south of Tampa, and it punches well above its weight. World-class beaches (Siesta Key has been rated #1 in the country), the Ringling Museum complex, a legitimate theater and arts scene, and enough good restaurants to fill a week. The circus wintered here, John Ringling built his mansion here, and the cultural institutions he seeded turned Sarasota into something more than a beach town.

Here's what's actually worth doing — from the beaches and the bayfront to the museums, the state parks, and the neighborhoods worth walking.

1. Siesta Key Beach

Siesta Key Beach has been rated the #1 beach in America multiple times, and the claim holds up. The sand is 99% quartz crystal — it stays cool underfoot even in summer, it's pure white, and it doesn't compact or clump. The beach is wide and long, the water is calm and shallow, and the drum circle every Sunday evening at sunset is one of the best free events in Florida. The public beach has restrooms, concessions, a volleyball area, and a large parking lot that fills by mid-morning in season.

Parking tip: The main Siesta Key Beach lot fills early. Arrive before 10 AM on weekends and holidays, or park on Beach Road and walk. The free Siesta Key Breeze trolley runs a loop around the island and connects to the mainland.

2. The Ringling

The Ringling is a 66-acre complex that includes the Museum of Art (one of the best in the Southeast), the Circus Museum, the Historic Asolo Theater, the Ca' d'Zan mansion (John and Mable Ringling's Venetian Gothic waterfront home), and the Bayfront Gardens. The art museum has a strong collection of Old Masters, including a gallery of massive Rubens paintings. Ca' d'Zan alone is worth the trip — a 36,000-square-foot mansion on Sarasota Bay that Ringling modeled after the Doge's Palace in Venice. The grounds are free to walk; museum admission covers everything else.

Free admission: The Ringling Museum of Art is free on Mondays for Florida residents. The grounds and gardens are always free to walk.

3. St. Armands Circle

St. Armands Circle is a round shopping and dining district on St. Armands Key, the small island between the mainland and Lido Key. John Ringling developed it in the 1920s and modeled the layout after a European piazza. Today it's upscale boutiques, galleries, and restaurants arranged around a landscaped circle with statues. It's a walkable afternoon — park once, shop, eat, and walk to Lido Beach a few blocks away. The restaurants range from casual (Columbia Restaurant) to high-end, and it's a popular sunset dinner spot.

4. Lido Key and Lido Beach

Lido Key is the barrier island between St. Armands and Siesta Key. Lido Beach is smaller and quieter than Siesta Key — the same white sand and calm Gulf water, but with fewer people. The south end of Lido Key (South Lido Park) has a nature trail, mangrove shoreline, and calm bay water on one side and Gulf beach on the other. It's a good spot for kayaking and paddleboarding. The north end of the beach is walking distance from St. Armands Circle.

5. Myakka River State Park

Myakka River is one of the largest and oldest state parks in Florida — 58 square miles of wetlands, prairies, and hardwood hammocks. The park has a canopy walkway (a suspension bridge 25 feet above the ground through the treetops), airboat tours on Upper Myakka Lake, and some of the best wildlife viewing in southwest Florida. Alligators are everywhere — the lake shore and the river bends are loaded. Kayaking the Myakka River is excellent, especially in the winter dry season when wildlife concentrates near the water.

6. Mote Marine Laboratory & Aquarium

Mote is a working marine research lab that's open to the public. It's smaller than a big-city aquarium but more focused — shark research, sea turtle rehabilitation, manatee care, and coral reef studies. The shark tank, the sea turtle hospital, and the touch tanks are the highlights. It's on City Island between the mainland and Lido Key, and you can combine it with a walk across the bridge to St. Armands Circle. Mote also runs boat-based eco tours and kayak trips from its campus.

7. Downtown Sarasota and Main Street

Downtown Sarasota is walkable and has more going on than you'd expect from a city of 60,000. Main Street has restaurants, boutiques, and galleries. The Sarasota Opera House, the Asolo Repertory Theatre, and the Florida Studio Theatre give the city a legitimate performing arts scene. The Saturday farmers market at Lemon Avenue and Main is one of the best in the state — year-round, with produce, prepared food, baked goods, and local vendors. The bayfront park is a short walk from Main Street.

8. Marie Selby Botanical Gardens

Selby Gardens sits on 15 acres along Sarasota Bay downtown and has one of the world's best collections of orchids and epiphytes (air plants). The waterfront setting is beautiful — Spanish moss, banyan trees, and bay views. The gardens recently completed a major renovation and expansion. It's a peaceful break from the beach, and the gift shop has a good selection of orchids if you want to bring one home. The downtown campus is the main attraction; a second location in Historic Spanish Point is worth a visit if you have extra time.

9. Sarasota Bay Dolphin Watching

Sarasota Bay has one of the most studied resident dolphin populations in the world — the Sarasota Dolphin Research Program has been tracking these pods since 1970. Several operators run dolphin cruises from the downtown marina and from Mote Marine. The dolphins here are used to boats and often approach closely. Sunset dolphin cruises on the bay with the downtown skyline behind you are a local favorite.

10. Kayaking and Paddleboarding

Sarasota has excellent paddling on both sides — the Gulf barrier islands and Sarasota Bay. The bay side of Lido Key and Siesta Key has calm water, mangrove tunnels, and reliable wildlife (dolphins, manatees, rays). The Myakka River is a longer paddle through wild interior Florida. Outfitters on Siesta Key and at Mote Marine rent kayaks and SUPs, and several run guided tours through the mangrove creeks. The morning calm before the sea breeze kicks in is the best time.

11. Longboat Key

Longboat Key is the barrier island north of Lido Key — 10 miles of quiet, upscale beach with no public parking lots (by design). It's a residential island with golf courses, upscale resorts, and a few excellent restaurants. The beach is accessible at a handful of public access points, but the limited parking keeps it uncrowded. If you're staying on Longboat Key, you have one of the most peaceful stretches of Gulf Coast beach in the state. The south end connects to St. Armands Circle.

12. Sarasota Jungle Gardens

Sarasota Jungle Gardens has been open since 1939 — a 10-acre tropical garden and small zoo with flamingos, parrots, alligators, and other animals in a garden setting. It's old-school Florida tourism, and that's part of the charm. The flamingo gardens, where the birds roam freely, are the highlight. It's a good stop for families with younger kids, and it takes about 90 minutes to walk the whole park.

13. Big Cat Habitat

Big Cat Habitat is a rescue sanctuary for big cats, bears, and primates in eastern Sarasota. It's not a zoo — the animals are rescues from circuses, private owners, and roadside attractions that can't be returned to the wild. The facility offers guided tours with the keepers, and the animals are housed in large natural enclosures. It's a Sarasota original tied to the city's circus heritage, and the proceeds go directly to animal care.

14. The Celery Fields

The Celery Fields is a 440-acre wetland restoration area in eastern Sarasota that's become one of the top birding spots in the southeastern US. Former celery farmland converted to stormwater treatment ponds, it now attracts hundreds of bird species — roseate spoonbills, wood storks, limpkins, and dozens of shorebird species. There's a hilltop overlook, paved trails, and boardwalks. It's free, open dawn to dusk, and worth the drive even if you're not a serious birder.

15. Oscar Scherer State Park

Oscar Scherer is a 1,400-acre state park between Sarasota and Venice with one of the last remaining populations of Florida scrub-jays — a threatened species found only in Florida. The park has kayaking and canoeing on South Creek, swimming in a freshwater lake, hiking trails through scrub and pine flatwoods, and good wildlife viewing. It's less well-known than Myakka River but smaller and more manageable for a half-day visit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Sarasota worth visiting?

Yes. World-class beaches (Siesta Key has been rated #1 in the country), the Ringling Museum complex, excellent restaurants, and more cultural institutions per capita than most Florida cities. It's the Gulf Coast destination for people who want beaches and culture in the same trip.

What is Sarasota best known for?

Siesta Key Beach, The Ringling Museum, the circus heritage (Ringling Brothers wintered here), St. Armands Circle, and a thriving arts and theater scene unusual for a city its size.

When is the best time to visit Sarasota?

March through May for warm weather and a full cultural calendar. October and November are the sweet spot — warm water, thin crowds, lower rates. Summer is hot with afternoon storms but prices drop and weekday mornings are quiet on the beaches.

How far is Sarasota from Tampa?

About 60 miles south, roughly one hour on I-75. From St. Pete Beach, about 45 minutes south. Sarasota has its own airport (SRQ) with direct flights from several major cities.

Is Sarasota good for families?

Very. Siesta Key Beach is calm and shallow, Mote Marine has sharks and sea turtles, Myakka River State Park has airboat rides and a canopy walkway, and the Saturday farmers market has enough food stalls to keep everyone happy.
