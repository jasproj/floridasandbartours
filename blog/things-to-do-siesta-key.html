12 Things to Do on Siesta Key

Sarasota County, Florida • Updated August 2026

Siesta Key is an 8-mile barrier island off Sarasota, connected by two bridges and famous for having the whitest, coolest sand in the country — 99% pure quartz crystal that doesn't heat up in the sun. The beach gets the headlines, but the island has a lot more going on than just sand.

Here's what's actually worth your time on Siesta Key, from the drum circle to the hidden snorkeling spot most tourists walk right past.

1. Siesta Key Beach

The main event. Siesta Key Beach (also called Siesta Beach) has been rated the #1 beach in the United States multiple times, and the hype is earned. The sand is pure quartz — white, powdery, and cool underfoot even at noon in July. The beach is wide, the water is calm and clear, and the facilities are excellent: restrooms, showers, a snack bar, picnic areas, a playground, and volleyball courts. Free parking, but the lot fills by 10 AM in season.

Parking tip: Arrive before 9 AM in season or use the overflow lot on Midnight Pass Road. The beach is worth the early start — afternoons are crowded and the lot closes when full.

2. The Sunday Drum Circle

Every Sunday evening, an hour or two before sunset, a drum circle forms on the beach near the main pavilion. It started decades ago and has become one of the most iconic traditions in Sarasota. Dozens of drummers, dancers, hula hoopers, fire performers, and several hundred spectators gather for a free, open-air jam session that runs until dark. Bring a drum, a blanket, or just yourself. It's the best free show on the Gulf Coast.

3. Point of Rocks

At the south end of Crescent Beach, a natural limestone formation called Point of Rocks juts into the Gulf. The rocks create tide pools and shallow reef areas where you can snorkel right from shore — no boat needed. Expect to see small tropical fish, sea urchins, crabs, and the occasional stingray. The water is calm and shallow, making it one of the best spots for beginners. Bring your own mask and snorkel — there are no rental shops at the rocks.

4. Siesta Village

The commercial hub of Siesta Key — a walkable stretch of Ocean Boulevard with restaurants, bars, ice cream shops, and boutiques. It's lively without being overwhelming, especially in the evening when people come off the beach and fill the outdoor patios. Key spots: the Daiquiri Deck for frozen drinks, Siesta Key Oyster Bar (SKOB) for live music, and The Hub Baja Grill for fish tacos. It's the social center of the island.

5. Crescent Beach

South of the main Siesta Beach, Crescent Beach is quieter and less crowded. The sand is the same quartz powder, but the beach is narrower and the parking is street-side instead of a big lot. It's where locals go when they want to avoid the main beach crowd. Point of Rocks is at the southern end, so you can combine a beach day with snorkeling.

6. Kayak and Paddleboard the Bay Side

The bay side of Siesta Key faces Sarasota Bay and Little Sarasota Bay — calmer, shallower water with mangrove islands, oyster bars, and seagrass flats. This is where you'll find manatees (especially in cooler months), dolphins, and wading birds. Several outfitters on the island rent kayaks and SUPs by the hour, or you can book a guided eco tour through the mangrove tunnels.

7. Turtle Beach

At the far south end of Siesta Key, Turtle Beach is the quietest and least visited of the three main beaches. It's rockier than Siesta Beach (more shell fragments, less quartz), but it has a boat ramp, a campground, and a playground. The water is shallower and warmer, and the fishing from the beach is productive. It's a good pick for families who want space and don't care about having the perfect sand.

8. Dolphin Boat Tours

Sarasota Bay is home to a resident pod of bottlenose dolphins that's been studied by the Mote Marine Lab for decades. Dolphin tours from Sarasota run through the bay and around the keys, and sighting rates are close to 100%. Smaller boats (6 passengers or fewer) give you a more intimate experience and can get into the shallow channels where dolphins feed.

9. Mote Marine Laboratory

Just across the bridge from Siesta Key, Mote Marine is a working research lab with a public aquarium. It has sharks, sea turtles, manatees, and a touch tank. The exhibits are focused on Gulf Coast marine life and the conservation work Mote does — it's more educational than entertainment, which makes it a good rainy-day option or a break from the beach. The new Mote SEA aquarium opened in 2026 with expanded exhibits and a shark habitat.

10. Big Olaf Creamery

A Siesta Key institution. Big Olaf makes their ice cream in-house with local cream, and the flavors rotate seasonally. The line out the door on a summer evening is part of the experience. It's on Ocean Boulevard in Siesta Village, so you can combine it with dinner and a walk. Cash and card accepted.

11. Fishing from the Bridges and Shoreline

The bridges connecting Siesta Key to the mainland are productive fishing spots, especially at night. Snook, redfish, sheepshead, and mangrove snapper are all common in these waters. The north bridge (Siesta Drive) and south bridge (Stickney Point Road) both have walkable catwalks. You don't need a boat to catch fish here — just a rod, some shrimp, and patience.

12. Day Trip to St. Armands Circle

A 15-minute drive north from Siesta Key, St. Armands Circle on Lido Key is a roundabout ringed with upscale shops, galleries, and restaurants. It's the place for a nice dinner, some window shopping, and a walk. Columbia Restaurant (Cuban-Spanish food, been there since the 1950s) is the anchor. After dinner, Lido Beach is a short walk — less crowded than Siesta and good for a sunset stroll.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Siesta Key Beach so famous?

The sand. It's 99% pure quartz crystal — stays cool in the sun, feels like powdered sugar, and has been rated #1 beach in the US multiple times. The beach is also wide, free, and has excellent facilities.

Is Siesta Key expensive?

Moderate by Florida beach standards. The beach is free with free parking (fills early in season). Vacation rentals run $150–400/night. Restaurants in Siesta Village are casual and reasonably priced.

What is the drum circle?

Every Sunday evening before sunset, a large drum circle forms on Siesta Key Beach near the main pavilion. Dozens of drummers, dancers, and spectators gather for a free jam session that runs until dark. It's been a tradition for decades.

Is Siesta Key good for snorkeling?

Point of Rocks at the south end of Crescent Beach is the spot — natural limestone formations with tide pools and small reef fish. Shallow, calm, and accessible from shore. Bring your own gear.

How far is Siesta Key from Sarasota?

About 15 minutes by car, connected by two bridges. Sarasota's restaurants, Ringling Museum, and Marie Selby Gardens are an easy day trip.
