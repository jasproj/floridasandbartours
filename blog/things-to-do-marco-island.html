12 Things to Do on Marco Island

Collier County, Florida • Updated August 2026

Marco Island is the biggest of Florida's Ten Thousand Islands — a 6-mile-long barrier island connected to the mainland by bridge, sitting 20 minutes south of Naples. It's quieter than most Gulf Coast beach towns, more residential than resort-driven, and backed by one of the largest undeveloped mangrove estuaries in North America. The beach is the draw, but the backcountry is where Marco Island separates from every other Florida destination.

Here's what's worth doing — from the main beach and Tigertail to the boat tours and fishing charters that make this corner of Florida unique.

Getting oriented: Marco Island is about 20 minutes south of Naples via Collier Blvd (SR-951). The main resort beach runs along the island's west side. Tigertail Beach is on the northwest. Goodland, the old fishing village on the south end, is the launch point for Ten Thousand Islands tours.

1. South Marco Beach (Crescent Beach)

The main beach on Marco Island is a wide crescent of white sand curving along the island's southwest shore. The JW Marriott and Hilton sit behind it, but the beach itself is public. South Marco Beach Access, at the south end of Collier Blvd, has parking (limited and metered), restrooms, and a short boardwalk to the sand. The water is warm, shallow, and calm. Walk south along the beach for quieter sand and better shelling — the shells pile up at the south tip where the current wraps around Cape Marco.

2. Tigertail Beach

Tigertail is the local's beach on the northwest side of the island. It's a county park with a parking lot ($8/day), restrooms, picnic shelters, a playground, and a kayak/paddleboard launch. The beach itself is split by a tidal lagoon — at low tide you can wade across to Sand Dollar Island, a sandbar that collects shells and sand dollars like a natural funnel. The lagoon is shallow and warm, perfect for young kids. Shorebirds (plovers, oystercatchers, terns) nest on the sand flats. Shelling here is consistently better than the main beach.

3. Ten Thousand Islands Boat Tours

The Ten Thousand Islands are a labyrinth of mangrove islands stretching south into Everglades National Park. Guided boat tours run from Goodland (Marco Island's south end) and from Everglades City to the east. You'll see dolphins, manatees, sea turtles, ospreys, bald eagles, and roseate spoonbills depending on the season. Some tours stop at uninhabited shell islands for shelling and swimming. The mangrove tunnels — narrow waterways arched with roots — are best by kayak. This is the wildest part of Florida, and it starts at Marco Island's back door.

4. Dolphin-Watching Cruises

Bottlenose dolphins are resident in the waters around Marco Island year-round. Most dolphin tours depart from the Goodland marina or the Rose Marina on the north end. Sightings are nearly guaranteed — the pods are well-known to the captains and they cruise the same passes and channels. Some operators combine dolphin watching with a shelling stop on a barrier island or a loop through the Ten Thousand Islands backcountry. Sunset dolphin cruises are the most popular booking.

5. Shelling

Marco Island is a serious shelling destination. The best spots are Tigertail Beach (Sand Dollar Island at low tide), the south tip of South Marco Beach, and Keewaydin Island (accessible only by boat). Common finds include sand dollars, fighting conchs, lightning whelks, murex, and lettered olives. The rules: low tide is best, early morning before other collectors, and after a storm or strong west wind is when the Gulf pushes the best shells ashore. Some shelling boat tours include stops at uninhabited barrier islands where the picking is better than any public beach.

6. Fishing Charters

Marco Island sits at the intersection of Gulf flats, Ten Thousand Islands backcountry, and offshore reef and wreck systems — which means the fishing is varied and year-round. Inshore charters target snook, redfish, spotted seatrout, and tarpon (spring and summer) in the mangrove creeks and grass flats. Offshore trips run to reefs and wrecks for grouper, snapper, cobia, and kingfish. Goodland is the hub for backcountry fishing guides who know the maze of channels through the Ten Thousand Islands. The bridge pilings and the passes between islands are productive spots you can fish from shore or kayak.

7. Kayaking and Paddleboarding

The mangrove waterways around Marco Island are some of the best paddling in Florida. Tigertail Beach has a kayak and SUP launch into the lagoon and surrounding mangroves. For a longer trip, guided kayak tours through the mangrove tunnels south of Goodland take you through a root-arched corridor where motorboats can't go. The water is flat, the wildlife is dense (herons, ibis, jumping mullet), and the quiet is the point. Paddleboard yoga and sunset paddles run out of the Rose Marina.

8. Goodland

Goodland is the old fishing village at the south end of Marco Island — a few hundred residents, a handful of waterfront restaurants, and the kind of Florida authenticity that the rest of the island has long since polished away. Stan's Idle Hour is the landmark bar: live music, cold beer, a crushed shell parking lot, and the Mullet Festival every January (the biggest event on this side of Collier County). The Little Bar is another local favorite. Goodland is also where most of the backcountry fishing guides and Ten Thousand Islands tour operators dock.

9. Briggs Nature Center

Part of the Rookery Bay National Estuarine Research Reserve, the Briggs Nature Center is on Shell Island Road between Naples and Marco Island. A half-mile boardwalk winds through coastal scrub and mangrove habitat. Guided nature walks, butterfly garden, and a small museum explaining the Ten Thousand Islands ecosystem. It's quiet and not many tourists know about it — good birding, good for kids, and a useful orientation to the natural environment before you take a boat tour into the backcountry.

10. Sunset Sailing

Sunset on the water off Marco Island is a different experience than watching from the beach. Several operators run sunset cruises — sail and power — from the Rose Marina and Goodland. The Marco Island Princess is a larger party-style boat; the smaller sailing catamarans offer a quieter ride. The western sky over the Gulf of Mexico on a clear evening puts on one of the best sunset shows on the coast. Dolphins often ride alongside in the passes as the boats head out.

11. Naples Day Trip

Naples is 20 minutes north and offers everything Marco Island doesn't have — upscale dining on Fifth Avenue South, the Naples Pier, shopping on Third Street South, the Naples Botanical Garden, and the Naples Zoo. It's an easy half-day trip: drive up for lunch and the pier, walk Fifth Avenue, and be back on the beach by sunset. The two destinations complement each other well — Marco for nature and quiet, Naples for dining and culture.

12. Cape Romano Dome Houses

South of Marco Island, partially submerged in the Gulf, sit the remnants of the Cape Romano dome houses — a cluster of concrete domes built in 1980 as a self-sustaining vacation home that erosion and hurricanes have slowly claimed. They're now mostly underwater and collapsing, but they remain one of the strangest sights on the Florida coast. You can see them from a boat tour or jet ski rental. They're iconic in Marco Island photos, and they won't last much longer — the Gulf is taking them a little more every year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Marco Island worth visiting?

Yes. If you want a quiet Gulf Coast beach with excellent shelling, easy access to the Everglades backcountry, and none of the spring-break energy of the Tampa Bay beaches, Marco Island is exactly right. It's more residential than resort-town, which is the appeal.

What is Marco Island known for?

Its crescent beach, Tigertail Beach and Sand Dollar Island, proximity to the Ten Thousand Islands, fishing, dolphin tours, and shelling. It's also the last developed island before the Everglades — everything south is mangroves and wilderness.

When is the best time to visit Marco Island?

November through April for dry, warm weather. February and March are the busiest. May is a sweet spot — warm water, thinner crowds, lower rates. Summer is hot and rainy but the water is bathtub-warm and prices drop.

Can you drive to Marco Island?

Yes. Two bridges via Collier Blvd (SR-951) connect it to the mainland. Twenty minutes from Naples, 50 minutes from Fort Myers, about 2 hours from Miami.

Is Marco Island good for families?

Very. Tigertail Beach's lagoon is shallow and safe for young kids, the beach is wide and calm, and the boat tours (dolphins, shelling islands, Ten Thousand Islands) are family favorites. The island is safe and easy to navigate.
