10 Things to Do in Tarpon Springs

Pinellas County, Florida • Updated August 2026

Tarpon Springs is a small city on Florida's Gulf Coast, about 40 minutes north of Tampa and 30 minutes from Clearwater Beach. It's famous for one thing: Greek sponge divers settled here in the early 1900s and built a community that still runs the waterfront. The Sponge Docks district is the main draw — Greek restaurants, working sponge boats, bakeries, and a waterfront that feels more Mediterranean than Floridian.

It's one of the best day trips on the Gulf Coast. Here's what's worth doing.

1. The Sponge Docks

A half-mile stretch of Dodecanese Boulevard along the Anclote River, lined with sponge shops, Greek restaurants, bakeries, and tourist shops. Working sponge boats still dock here, and you can watch divers bring up natural sponges the way they've done it for over a century. The atmosphere is part Greek village, part Florida waterfront — and it works. Walk the docks, buy a natural sponge (they're actually excellent), and eat Greek food until you can't move.

2. Greek Food

Tarpon Springs has more Greek restaurants per capita than anywhere in Florida, and the quality is high because the competition is fierce. The essential stops: Hellas Restaurant for a full sit-down meal (saganaki — the flaming cheese — is a must-order), Mykonos for lamb and seafood, and Mr. Souvlaki for a quick gyro. Don't skip the bakeries — loukoumades (Greek doughnuts drizzled in honey) and baklava are sold on every block. The food alone justifies the trip.

Timing tip: Arrive for a late lunch (2–3 PM) to avoid the worst crowds. Weekend evenings are packed — weekday visits are more relaxed.

3. Sponge Diving Demonstration Cruise

Several operators run short boat cruises from the Sponge Docks that include a live sponge diving demonstration. A diver goes down in an old-style diving suit and brings up sponges from the bottom while the captain narrates the history of the industry. The cruises are typically 30–45 minutes and are one of the most unique boat experiences on the Gulf Coast. It's educational, it's entertaining, and kids love it.

4. Anclote Key Preserve State Park

Anclote Key is a barrier island about 3 miles offshore from Tarpon Springs, accessible only by boat. It has a Gulf-side beach with excellent shelling and swimming, a historic lighthouse (built 1887, not climbable but photogenic), and no development — no restrooms, no concessions, just beach and nature. Several boat tour operators run daily trips from the Tarpon Springs area. Bring water, food, sunscreen, and a snorkel if you want to explore the shallow grass flats.

5. Spongeorama Sponge Factory

A free museum/shop on the Sponge Docks that shows the full history of the Tarpon Springs sponge industry through a short film, old diving equipment displays, and a room full of sponge products. It's not big, but it's free and gives you context for what you're seeing on the docks. The film is surprisingly good — it covers the Greek immigration, the red tide that nearly wiped out the industry, and how natural sponge diving works today.

6. Historic Downtown Tarpon Springs

Most visitors stick to the Sponge Docks, but historic downtown Tarpon Springs — about a mile south on Tarpon Avenue — is worth a walk. It has antique shops, art galleries, the old train depot, and the Tarpon Springs Cultural Center. The architecture is a mix of Greek revival and early Florida, and the pace is slow. If the Sponge Docks are the tourist draw, downtown is where the town actually lives.

7. St. Nicholas Greek Orthodox Cathedral

A landmark Greek Orthodox church built in 1943, modeled after the Hagia Sophia in Istanbul. The interior has marble imported from Greece, hand-painted icons, and stained glass. It's the site of the annual Epiphany celebration every January 6th, when a cross is thrown into Spring Bayou and young men dive in to retrieve it — one of the largest Epiphany celebrations in the Western Hemisphere. The church is open to visitors outside of services.

8. Sunset Beach

Tarpon Springs' public beach, at the west end of Sunset Drive. It's small and local — no resorts, no lifeguards, just a strip of sand on the Gulf with a park and picnic tables behind it. The sunset views are excellent (it faces due west), and it's far less crowded than Clearwater or St. Pete Beach. Good for a low-key beach afternoon combined with a Sponge Docks visit.

9. Kayak the Anclote River

The Anclote River runs through Tarpon Springs and empties into the Gulf near the Sponge Docks. The upper river has mangroves, wading birds, and tidal creeks — quiet paddling territory that feels nothing like the tourist waterfront a few miles downstream. Several outfitters rent kayaks and paddleboards. For a longer trip, paddle from the river mouth out to Anclote Key — about 3 miles one way in open water, for experienced paddlers only.

10. Fred Howard Park

A county park on a causeway that leads to a small manmade island with a beach. The beach is calm (bay side, not Gulf), the water is shallow and warm, and the causeway walk is scenic. It's popular with local families and a good alternative to the Gulf beaches when the surf is rough. Free admission, small parking fee. The sunsets from the causeway are outstanding.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Tarpon Springs known for?

Its Greek heritage and the natural sponge industry. Greek sponge divers settled here in the early 1900s, and the Sponge Docks district preserves that culture with restaurants, bakeries, sponge shops, and working sponge boats. Highest percentage of Greek Americans of any US city.

Is Tarpon Springs worth a day trip?

Yes — one of the best day trips from Tampa or Clearwater. The Sponge Docks, Greek food, Anclote Key boat tours, and historic downtown fill a full day. About 40 minutes from Tampa, 30 from Clearwater.

What should I eat?

Greek food. Hellas or Mykonos for a sit-down meal (order the saganaki). Loukoumades and baklava from any bakery on the docks. Mr. Souvlaki for a quick gyro. Rusty Bellies for Gulf seafood.

Can you swim at Anclote Key?

Yes. Anclote Key has a beach with good swimming, shelling, and snorkeling. No facilities on the island — bring everything. Boat tours run daily from Tarpon Springs.

Is Tarpon Springs free?

Walking the Sponge Docks, shopping, and exploring downtown are free. You pay for food, boat tours, and sponge diving demo cruises. Parking is metered but cheap.
